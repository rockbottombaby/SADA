SADA

Track the flights that carry weapons.

A free, open-source tool that helps journalists and researchers trace weapons flights and hold the governments behind them to account.

Open SADA on a computer

SADA is built for a large screen: a map, a search panel and a research assistant side by side. Phones aren't supported yet.

What is SADA ↓

Why it exists

States use large-scale data and AI-assisted analysis to wage war. SADA turns the same kind of capability toward accountability. Flight-tracking sites show what is in the air now; SADA answers the questions reporting needs: which flights carried weapons, through whose airspace, and who let them pass.

What you can do

  • Find flightsBetween countries or airports, through any country's airspace, or by aircraft and airline. Every flight since March 2023.
  • See the detailsA flight's route, which countries it flew over and when, and what the aircraft did before and after.
  • Watch live trafficMilitary aircraft worldwide, as they fly.
  • Ask the assistantIn plain language. It investigates, shows each step on the map and gives a source for every number.

How it works

  1. Volunteers' receivers around the world pick up the signals aircraft broadcast.
  2. Every day, SADA turns that archive into flights, over 120 million so far, and works out which countries' airspace each one crossed.
  3. You search it in your browser. Nothing you look up is stored or tracked.

Flight data never shows cargo. SADA shows which flights to ask about.

In memory

Sha'ban Ahmad al-Dalou (2004–2024), a young Palestinian software engineering student, burned alive when Israel bombed an emergency tent camp in Deir al-Balah, Gaza. SADA takes its name from his initials.
