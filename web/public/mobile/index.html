The AI research assistant

Which military and cargo flights flew through a country's airspace on their way to a war zone this year, and who operated them?
SADA
  1. Checks where receivers could hear aircraft that year, and where they couldn't
  2. Pulls out every flight that crossed that airspace, and separates seen from estimated
  3. Picks out the military and cargo flights and reads each one's full record
  4. Compares the months with US arms-export figures and searches the press
  5. Draws every step on the map, then answers

Why it exists

States use large-scale data and AI-assisted analysis to wage war. SADA turns the same kind of capability toward accountability. Flight-tracking sites show what is in the air now; SADA answers the questions reporting needs: which military and cargo flights flew where, through whose airspace, and who let them pass.

What you can do

  • Ask the AIAny question in plain language. It runs the investigation for you and shows its sources.
  • Search yourselfFlights between countries or airports, through any country's airspace, or by aircraft, airline or callsign.
  • Open any flightIts route, which countries' airspace it crossed and when, and the aircraft's other flights.
  • Watch live trafficMilitary aircraft worldwide, and everything else in the area you are looking at.

How it works

  1. Volunteers' receivers around the world pick up the position signals aircraft broadcast. The ADSB.lol network publishes them daily.
  2. Every day, SADA turns them into flights, over 120 million since March 2023, and works out which countries' airspace each one crossed.
  3. You ask, the AI investigates, right in your browser. Nothing you look up is stored or tracked.
Seen — a receiver heard the aircraft inside that airspace.
Estimated — it was heard before and after a gap, and its path across the gap crosses the airspace.
Flight data never shows what was on board. SADA shows which flights to ask about; records requests show the rest.

In memory

Sha'ban Ahmad al-Dalou (2004–2024), a young Palestinian software engineering student, burned alive when Israel bombed an emergency tent camp in Deir al-Balah, Gaza. SADA takes its name from his initials.
