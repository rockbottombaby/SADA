SADA Source code

Track the flights that carry weapons.

States use large-scale data and AI-assisted analysis to wage war. SADA turns the same kind of capability toward accountability: a free, open-source tool that helps journalists and researchers trace the flights that carry weapons, and hold the governments that ship them, or let them pass, to account.

Open SADA Source code

What you can do

  • Find flights between two countries or airports, through a country's airspace, or by aircraft, airline or callsign. Every flight since March 2023.
  • Open any flight to see its route, which countries it flew over and when, and the aircraft's other flights.
  • Watch live traffic, including military aircraft worldwide.
  • Ask the assistant in plain language. It investigates, shows each step on the map and gives a source for every number. It runs on your own Anthropic key.

How to read the results

  • Observed — a receiver saw the aircraft inside that airspace.
  • Inferred — its path across a gap in coverage crosses the airspace. An estimate, not a sighting.
  • No data does not mean no flights. Where there are no receivers, nothing is recorded. Flight data never shows cargo.

Free and private

Searching, the map and live traffic are free. The assistant is billed to your own Anthropic account: cents for a simple question, a dollar or two for a long investigation. There are no accounts and nothing about you is stored.

In memory

Sha'ban Ahmad al-Dalou (2004–2024), a young Palestinian software engineering student, burned alive when Israel bombed an emergency tent camp for families displaced by the genocide, in Deir al-Balah, Gaza, on October 14, 2024.

SADA takes its name from his initials, S.A.D., with A for Airwatch; sada (صدى) also means “echo” in Arabic.
